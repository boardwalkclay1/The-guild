🕯️ Candlestick Mastery

The Guild reads candles like language. Every wick, every body, every gap is a sentence in the story of price.

Below are the core formations you must know — not as trivia, but as signals on the battlefield.

Core Single Candles
🔨
Hammer
Bullish reversal • Buyers reclaim control after a selloff
›

Structure: Small body near the top, long lower wick, little to no upper wick.

Story: Sellers pushed price down, but buyers stepped in aggressively and closed price near the highs.

• Most powerful after a clear downtrend into support.

• Confirmation comes from the next candle closing higher.

Guild Note: A hammer at random means nothing. A hammer at a key level is a signal.
🌠
Shooting Star
Bearish reversal • Buyers exhausted into resistance
›

Structure: Small body near the bottom, long upper wick, little to no lower wick.

Story: Buyers pushed price up, but sellers slammed it back down near the open.

• Most meaningful after a strong push into resistance or an extended uptrend.

• Confirmation is a strong red candle after the star.

Guild Note: A shooting star at a daily level is a warning flare — respect it.
⚖️
Doji
Indecision • Balance between buyers and sellers
›

Structure: Open and close are nearly the same, with wicks above and/or below.

Story: Neither side could dominate. Momentum is pausing, not yet choosing a direction.

• At the top of a trend, it can signal a potential reversal.

• In the middle of a range, it often just confirms chop.

Guild Note: A doji is a question mark. The next candle is the answer.
🚀
Marubozu
Full conviction • Strong trend candle
›

Structure: Long body with almost no wicks — either full green or full red.

Story: One side dominated from open to close with little opposition.

• A green marubozu in a breakout can signal strong continuation.

• A red marubozu through support can mark a breakdown.

Guild Note: Marubozu candles are momentum flags — trade with them, not against them.
Multi‑Candle Formations
🟩🟥
Bullish Engulfing
Bullish reversal • Buyers overpower prior selling
›

Structure: A small red candle followed by a larger green candle that fully engulfs the prior body.

Story: Sellers had control, but buyers stepped in and erased the prior candle’s move.

• Best after a downtrend into support.

• Volume confirmation strengthens the signal.

Guild Note: Engulfing patterns are power shifts. Respect the side that just took control.
🟥🟩
Bearish Engulfing
Bearish reversal • Sellers overpower prior buying
›

Structure: A small green candle followed by a larger red candle that fully engulfs the prior body.

Story: Buyers tried to push higher, but sellers crushed the move and took control.

• Best after an extended push into resistance.

• Often marks the start of a pullback or reversal.

Guild Note: At daily resistance, a bearish engulfing is a serious warning.
🌅
Morning Star
Bullish reversal • Night gives way to dawn
›

Structure: Strong red candle, then a small indecision candle, then a strong green candle.

Story: Selling pressure weakens, balance appears, then buyers reclaim control.

• Most powerful at major support after a downtrend.

• The third candle closing above the midpoint of the first is key.

Guild Note: The Morning Star is a classic bottoming pattern — but only at real levels.
🌇
Evening Star
Bearish reversal • Day fades into night
›

Structure: Strong green candle, then a small indecision candle, then a strong red candle.

Story: Buying pressure stalls, balance appears, then sellers take over.

• Most powerful at major resistance after an uptrend.

• The third candle closing below the midpoint of the first confirms weakness.

Guild Note: The Evening Star is a ceiling pattern — treat it as a warning to tighten risk.
🌀
Spinning Top
Low conviction • Short‑term indecision
›

Structure: Small body with upper and lower wicks of similar length.

Story: Price moved both ways, but closed near the middle — no side truly won.

• In trends, can signal a pause before continuation or reversal.

• Needs context — level, volume, and next candle.

Guild Note: Spinning tops are whispers, not shouts. Listen, but don’t overreact.
